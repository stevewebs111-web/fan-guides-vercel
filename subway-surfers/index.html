Subway Surfers, World Tour Cities and Hoverboards

Subway Surfers is an endless runner where you sprint along train tracks, dodge trains and grab coins while a guard chases you. What keeps it fresh after all these years is the World Tour, a new city every few weeks with its own character, board and look. This guide covers the tour, the hoverboards and the habits that turn good runs into great ones.

The World Tour keeps the game moving

Every few weeks the game travels to a new city, from Paris and Tokyo to Cairo, Rio and Sydney, and the tracks, the background and the music change with it. Each stop brings a themed character and a themed hoverboard to unlock, and collecting them is a big part of the fun for long term players. The cities also change how a run feels, because the obstacles and the layout shift a little with each new place. If you play a little every day during a tour stop, you will usually earn enough to unlock that city's character before the tour moves on. Hoverboards are your safety net

A hoverboard lets you survive one crash, which makes it the most valuable item in the game. Some boards add small bonuses too, like extra speed or a smoother landing, and the limited boards from tour stops are the ones collectors want most. The best habit is to save boards for runs where you are already beating your personal best, because a board used on a slow run is a board wasted. Upgrading boards in the shop is worth it once you have a few, because a longer lasting board covers more of the fast, dangerous part of a run.

Characters, coins and keys

You start as Jake, and coins and keys unlock a large cast, each with their own outfit and style. Characters are mostly cosmetic, they do not change how fast you run, so pick the one you like looking at. Coins line the tracks and pay for characters, boards and upgrades. Keys revive you after a crash, and they get more expensive each time you use them in one run, so the first revive is cheap and the later ones are not. Save keys for runs that are already beating your best, not for the first crash of a slow run.

Power-ups and missions

Four power-ups appear during runs. The jetpack flies you above the tracks collecting coins. Super sneakers let you jump higher. The coin magnet pulls coins toward you. The 2x multiplier doubles your score. Upgrading power-ups makes them last longer, and the multiplier is the best first upgrade because it pays off on every run. Daily missions give you targets like collecting a number of coins or jumping over a set number of barriers, and finishing them earns rewards, so a short daily session builds up coins faster than one long session now and then.

Tips for longer runs

Stay near the middle lane when you can, because it gives you the most escape options. Look two obstacles ahead, not at your character, because the speed makes reaction time short. A quick roll under a low barrier is often safer than a jump over it. Learn the rhythm of the ramps, because landing on a train roof is usually safer than staying on the ground.
