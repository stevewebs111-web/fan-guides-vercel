Hill Climb Racing, Vehicles and Stages Guide

Hill Climb Racing looks like a simple driving game, but behind it there is a garage full of vehicles and a long list of stages, each with its own physics. This guide walks through the vehicles, the stages and the upgrade choices that make the biggest difference to how far you drive.

Your garage, vehicle by vehicle

You begin with the jeep, a forgiving little vehicle that teaches you the controls. Coins unlock more than 30 others, and each one drives differently. The motocross bike is light and jumps well but flips easily. The monster truck powers over rough ground. The tank is slow but almost impossible to stop. The race car is fast on flat stages and tricky on hills. There is no single best vehicle, because each stage rewards different strengths, so the smart move is to keep two or three vehicles upgraded and switch between them depending on where you are driving. The hill climb racing mod fan guide explains each vehicle in plain words, which helps when you are deciding where to spend your coins next.

Stages change the rules

The first stage, Countryside, is gentle rolling hills. After that the game changes the rules with every stage. The Desert has soft sand that slows you down. The Moon has low gravity, so you float further with every jump. The Arctic has ice, so braking distances grow. The Highway rewards pure speed and punishes rough driving. Learning one stage well is worth more than rushing through them all, because the coins you earn on a stage you know pay for the upgrades you need on the next one.

Upgrades, what to buy first

Every vehicle has four upgrade paths, engine, tires, suspension and fuel. The engine gives you power for steep climbs. Tires give you grip on loose or icy ground. Suspension keeps you stable when you land. Fuel gives you more time between cans. A good order for a new player is tires first, then fuel, then engine, then suspension, because grip and fuel keep you alive long enough to learn the stage. Once you know a stage, the engine becomes the best investment, because speed turns into distance, and distance turns into coins.

Fuel cans and distance

Fuel cans sit along the road, and the gap between them grows the further you go. The fuel gauge is the real clock of every run, so watch it as closely as the road. If you are about to run dry, slow down and roll, because coasting uses almost no fuel and the next can may be just over the next hill. Hard acceleration on steep ground burns fuel fast, so a steady pace often beats a fast one on the stages with long gaps.

Small habits, longer runs

Land with all four wheels down when you can, and use the pedals in the air to keep the nose level. Brake in the air if the front is too high. Do not hold the gas flat out on every crest, a short lift often keeps you grounded and saves fuel. Play the daily events, because they pay well and teach you stages you might otherwise ignore.
